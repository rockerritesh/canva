Parts on this plate

  1. 1SoC (Helio G80) under the metal RF shield. The phone's main processor.
  2. 2PMIC, the power chip. Reads the thermistor voltage and decides whether the phone may keep running.
  3. 3Charging IC. Sets charging current based on the temperature the PMIC reports.
  4. 4Battery connector (BTB). Carries battery +, −, and the thermistor line. A loose or corroded one causes the same symptoms as a bad sensor.
  5. 5T1 · Board thermistor. Near the charger and connector, watches board heat.
  6. 6T2 · Battery thermistor. On the battery's own protection board. This is the main battery temperature sensor.
  7. 7Battery BN54, 5020 mAh. Replacing it also replaces T2.
  8. 8Main flex cable linking the main board to the sub-board.
  9. 9Sub-board with USB-C port, microphone and loudspeaker.

What the sensor is

A thermistor is a tiny resistor whose resistance changes with heat. An NTC type has lower resistance when hot and higher when cold. The phone measures that resistance to know the battery temperature.

How a bad sensor causes a bootloop

The thermistor and a fixed resistor form a voltage divider. The PMIC reads the middle voltage and converts it to a temperature. If the sensor breaks, the voltage is wrong, the phone thinks the battery is dangerous, and it cuts power during boot. Change the temperature and the fault below to see what the phone sees.
